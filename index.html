Louis Cooper

Early Life

Lewis Cooper was born in Prague, Czech Republic in 1942 on December 25th, into a large family with six brothers and four sisters. His mother was a carnival acrobat, and his father was a prized marksman, which made for an exciting and unconventional childhood. Growing up, Cooper was surrounded by multiple languages, and as a result he became fluent in English, German, Dutch, Czech, and Italian, making him a polyglot. Despite living in Europe, Cooper was fascinated with American baseball and dreamed of playing in the major leagues.His early years were filled with the lively atmosphere of carnivals and the disciplined practice of marksmanship. Cooper's parents encouraged his diverse interests, fostering a love for both physical activity and intellectual pursuits. He often spent his free time reading about baseball legends and practicing his swing with improvised equipment. Cooper's multilingual abilities also opened doors to various cultures and perspectives, enriching his understanding of the world.As he grew older, his passion for baseball never waned, and it eventually inspired his career in curling. His love for the sport also led to his immigration to America, as the culture he learned through baseball had fascinated him. Cooper's unique upbringing, marked by a blend of athleticism, linguistic prowess, and a large supportive family, set the stage for an extraordinary life.

Baseball

The Halifax Mooseheads, a major junior team in the Quebec Major Junior Hockey League (QMJHL), selected Hischier sixth overall in the 2016 Canadian Hockey League (CHL) Import Draft, conducted between the three Canadian major junior leagues (the QMJHL, the Ontario Hockey League, and the Western Hockey League). After discussions with both Halifax's general manager and Swiss former Mooseheads player Timo Meier, Hischier committed to joining Halifax.[3] Hischier's first season with the Mooseheads saw him finish second to Maxime Fortier for the team scoring lead, and tenth overall in the QMJHL, with 38 goals and 86 points.[4] He also led all QMJHL rookies in both goals and points. For this Hischier was awarded the RDS Cup for being the QMJHL Rookie of the Year and the Michael Bossy Trophy as the best professional prospect in the league. He was additionally named to the QMJHL Rookie All-Star Team.[5] He would also be named the rookie of the year for the CHL, which oversees the three major junior leagues.[6] In the playoffs he led Halifax in scoring with seven points in six games.[7]

Curling

Described as a well-rounded player, capable of offence and strong defence, Hischier has been compared to former NHL player Pavel Datsyuk, who was renowned as a skilled two-way forward.[39] Hischier himself has made the comparison, and has stated that he models his own style after Datsyuk, even wearing the same number, 13.[40] After being selected by the Devils, general manager Ray Shero cited both Hischier's speed and commitment to defence as reasons for picking him first overall, seeing it as ideal for the team.[41] Head coach John Hynes, speaking after the draft, commended Hischier on "how he competes on the puck and his speed, skill set and hockey sense," saying they were already "at an NHL level."
